An ISBN, or International Standard Book Number, is a product number for a book. It tells shops, libraries and distributors exactly which edition and format they're dealing with, and who published it. You need one for most printed books sold through shops and distributors. Each format needs its own number, and you get them from the official ISBN agency in the country where you, the publisher, are based.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Rules and prices differ by country, so check with your national ISBN agency.

## In short

- An ISBN identifies one edition in one format. It is not copyright.
- Paperback, hardback, ebook and audiobook each need a different ISBN.
- Amazon KDP gives free ISBNs for paperbacks and hardcovers, but they only work on KDP.
- Ebooks on Amazon don't need an ISBN.
- Buy ISBNs from your country's official agency, and only there.

## What is an ISBN for?

An ISBN is, in the words of the International ISBN Agency, "essentially a product identifier used by publishers, booksellers, libraries, internet retailers and other supply chain participants". When a bookshop orders your book, a library adds it to its catalogue or a distributor lists it, the ISBN is how they make sure it's your book, in the right format.

It doesn't protect your work. Bowker, the US agency, is clear that "the ISBN conveys no legal or copyright protection". Your copyright comes from the law in your country.

## Do you need an ISBN?

It depends on how and where you'll sell the book.

| How you'll publish | Do you need an ISBN? |
| --- | --- |
| Kindle ebook on Amazon KDP | No. KDP says it's optional for ebooks |
| Paperback or hardcover on Amazon KDP only | Yes, but KDP offers a free one |
| Print book sold through bookshops, libraries or other online stores | Yes, and it's best to own it yourself |
| Books sold to schools or institutions | Usually yes, as they often order by ISBN |
| A small print run you sell only by hand | Not strictly, but it helps if you ever want to sell more widely |

## Why does each format need its own ISBN?

Because a shop needs to know which one it's ordering. KDP says "Each format of your book (paperback vs hardcover) needs its own ISBN", and the International ISBN Agency says each product form "(e.g. paperback, EPUB, .pdf) should be identified separately".

A new edition with real changes to the text also needs a new ISBN. Fixing a few typos usually doesn't, but check your agency's rules.

## Should you use a free ISBN from Amazon KDP?

KDP offers free ISBNs for paperbacks and hardcovers. They're convenient, but they come with two limits you should understand first:

1. **They only work on KDP.** KDP says its free ISBNs "can only be used on KDP" and "cannot be used to publish outside of KDP". If you later want the same book in other shops, you'll need a different ISBN for that version.
2. **Your book's publisher will show as "Independently published".** KDP says this is how the imprint appears when you use its free ISBN.

If you only plan to sell on Amazon, a free KDP ISBN is often fine. If you want your book in bookshops, libraries or schools under your own publishing name, buy your own. I compare the two main print platforms in [KDP or IngramSpark?](/blog/kdp-or-ingramspark)

## Where do you buy an ISBN?

From the official ISBN agency for the country where you, as the publisher, are based. Each country has one. Buying from anyone else risks a number that doesn't list you as the publisher.

| Country | Official agency | Price (checked 21 November 2026) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| United Kingdom and Ireland | Nielsen ISBN Store | £93 for 1; £174 for a block of 10 |
| United States | Bowker (MyIdentifiers) | $125 for 1; $295 for 10 |
| Nigeria | National Library of Nigeria (Nigerian ISBN Agency) | Check the agency's site; fees are set by the National Library |
| Anywhere else | Find yours on the International ISBN Agency's directory | Varies |

Notice how much cheaper ISBNs are in blocks. If you plan more than one book, or more than one format, a block usually works out cheaper per number. ISBNs don't expire, so spare ones stay usable.

## What should you do once you have an ISBN?

1. **Assign it to one format** of one book, and record which is which.
2. **Register the book's details** with your agency, as they ask. This is how shops and libraries find the information.
3. **Print it on the book**: on the copyright page, and as a barcode on the back cover of print books.
4. **Use the same number everywhere** that format is listed.

Keep a simple spreadsheet of every ISBN you own and what it's assigned to. It saves confusion later.

This is general guidance, not legal advice. Rules differ by country, so check with the official agency or a lawyer for your situation.
