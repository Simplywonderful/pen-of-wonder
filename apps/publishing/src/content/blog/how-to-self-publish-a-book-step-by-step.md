Self-publishing means you act as the publisher. You hire the people a publisher would, you pay for each step, and you keep control of the book and its rights. The work comes in nine steps: finish the manuscript, get it edited, design the cover, lay out the inside, get ISBNs, choose how to print, check a proof copy, set up the listings, and plan how readers will find the book. Here's each one in order.

## In short

- You do a publisher's job, so you pay a publisher's costs: editing, design, layout and printing.
- Don't skip editing or the proof copy. They're where most self-published books go wrong.
- Get your own ISBNs if you want the book in shops and libraries.
- Plan how readers will find the book before it's printed, not after.

## Step 1: Finish the manuscript

Finish the whole book before you spend money on anything else. Then put it away for a couple of weeks and read it again with fresh eyes. You'll see things you couldn't see while you were writing it.

If you can, ask two or three people who are like your intended readers to read it and tell you where they got bored or confused.

## Step 2: Get it edited

Editing comes in three main kinds, and a book may need one, two or all three:

| Kind of edit | What it fixes | When you need it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Developmental or structural edit | The big picture: plot, structure, pacing, what to cut | When the book doesn't yet work as a whole |
| Copy edit | Sentences: clarity, consistency, grammar, word choice | When the structure is right but the writing needs tightening |
| Proofread | Final errors: typos, punctuation, layout slips | Always, as the last check before printing |

Ask an editor for a sample edit of a few pages before you commit. It shows you how they work, and whether you agree with their changes.

## Step 3: Design the cover

The cover is the first thing a reader judges, often as a small image on a phone screen. Hire a designer who has made covers in your genre, and look at the best-selling books like yours before you brief them.

You'll need a front cover for ebooks, and a full wrap (back, spine and front) for print. The spine width depends on the page count and paper, so the full cover is finished after the layout.

## Step 4: Lay out the inside

Layout, also called typesetting or interior design, turns your manuscript into pages: the right trim size, margins, fonts, chapter openings, page numbers and front matter such as the title and copyright pages.

For a novel, a careful layout is mostly invisible; readers only notice when it's bad. For a picture book, the layout is where words and illustrations come together on every spread, so it needs a designer who works with illustrated books.

## Step 5: Get your ISBNs

You'll need a separate ISBN for each format: paperback, hardback, ebook and audiobook. Buy them from the official ISBN agency in your country. Amazon KDP offers free ISBNs for print books, but they can only be used on KDP. I explain the options in [What is an ISBN, and do you need one?](/blog/what-is-an-isbn-and-do-you-need-one).

## Step 6: Choose how to print

You have two main options:

- **Print on demand.** Each copy is printed when someone orders it, through platforms such as Amazon KDP or IngramSpark. No stock to store, and nothing to pay up front, but a higher cost per copy.
- **A print run.** You order a few hundred or more copies from a printer. A lower cost per copy, but you pay up front and need somewhere to keep them.

Which one fits depends on where you'll sell. If you're not sure, read [KDP or IngramSpark?](/blog/kdp-or-ingramspark) for the print-on-demand side.

## Step 7: Check a printed proof

Order a printed proof copy and read every page of it. Screens hide problems that paper shows: colours that print darker than expected, text too close to the edge, a page out of order, a spine that's slightly off.

Check:

- [ ] Cover colours, spine text and barcode
- [ ] Margins, especially near the spine
- [ ] Page order and page numbers
- [ ] Image quality in illustrated books
- [ ] Every page of text, one last time

Don't approve the book for sale until the proof is right.

## Step 8: Set up your listings

Your book's listing is its shop window. For each platform, you'll write:

- a title and subtitle that tell readers what the book is
- a description that makes them want to read it, often your back-cover blurb
- categories and keywords that help the right readers find it
- a price that covers your printing costs and leaves you something

Write the description as carefully as the first page of the book. It's what people read before they decide.

## Step 9: Plan how readers will find it

A book doesn't sell because it exists. Before it's printed, decide who it's for and how they'll hear about it: your own network, events and readings, schools or churches, bookshops, an email list, social media, or paid ads.

Start with the readers you can reach directly. A first book usually sells through people who already know or trust the author, and grows from there.

## What does self-publishing cost?

It depends heavily on the book. A short, text-only book needs editing, a cover and layout. A full-colour picture book also needs illustrations, which are often the biggest cost. Get at least two quotes for each service, and ask exactly what's included, such as how many rounds of changes.

Uploading to KDP or IngramSpark is free. The money goes on the people who make the book good.
