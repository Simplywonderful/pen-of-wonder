If most of your sales will be on Amazon, Amazon KDP (Kindle Direct Publishing) is the simplest place to print your book. If you want bookshops, libraries and schools to be able to order it, IngramSpark is built for that. Many self-publishers use both: KDP for Amazon, IngramSpark for everywhere else. The choice depends on where your readers buy books.

## In short

- **KDP**: free to use, sells on Amazon, simple royalty formula, free ISBNs that only work on KDP.
- **IngramSpark**: free to upload, distributes to a wide network of shops and libraries, charges a 1% market access fee on distributed books.
- **Both together**: common, and sensible if you want reach beyond Amazon. Use your own ISBNs.
- Prices and terms change. Check both sites before you decide.

## How do KDP and IngramSpark compare?

| | Amazon KDP | IngramSpark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost to upload | Free | Free, according to IngramSpark |
| Where it sells | Amazon stores; Expanded Distribution is optional | A global network IngramSpark says reaches over 40,000 retailers, libraries, universities and educators |
| ISBN | Free KDP ISBN available for print, usable only on KDP; or your own | Your own |
| Revisions | Free | Free within 60 days of first production, then a fee |
| Distribution fee | Built into the royalty | Market access fee of 1% of list price on distributed books |
| Best for | Books that will mostly sell on Amazon | Books you want in shops, libraries and schools |

## How do KDP paperback royalties work?

KDP gives a clear formula: "(Royalty rate x list price) – printing costs = royalty". On Amazon.com, the rate is 60% for paperbacks listed at 9.99 US dollars or more, and 50% below that. Other Amazon marketplaces have their own thresholds.

KDP's own example: a 15 dollar book with 5 dollars of printing costs earns (0.60 × 15) − 5 = 4 dollars per sale.

Printing costs depend on page count, trim size and whether the inside is in colour. Children's picture books, printed in full colour, cost much more to print than novels, so check the cost before you set your price.

## What does IngramSpark charge?

IngramSpark says "it costs you nothing to sign up for an IngramSpark account and to upload your print or ebook". When it removed its setup fees in 2023, it also introduced two terms worth knowing:

- **Revisions** are free "within 60 days of the book's first production date". After that, a fee applies.
- **A market access fee** "equivalent to 1% of the local list price at the time of sale" is charged on books enabled for its global distribution.

Bookshops buy at a discount, which you set when you list the book. The discount affects both your earnings and whether shops are willing to stock it. Read IngramSpark's current pricing guide before you choose.

## Can you use both?

Yes. A common setup is:

1. **KDP** for the Amazon edition, so Amazon prints and ships it directly.
2. **IngramSpark** for the same book, with distribution switched on, so other retailers and libraries can order it.

If you do this, use your own ISBN rather than KDP's free one ([what an ISBN is, and where to buy one](/blog/what-is-an-isbn-and-do-you-need-one)). KDP's free ISBNs "can only be used on KDP". Check each platform's help pages for how to set up the same book on both without conflicts.

## Which should you choose?

Ask where your readers will buy the book.

- **Mostly on Amazon, from people who find you online**: start with KDP.
- **In bookshops, libraries or schools**: you need a route they can order through. IngramSpark is one, or a local printer and distributor.
- **Both**: use both, with your own ISBNs.
- **Mostly at events, churches or schools you visit**: a short print run from a local printer may cost less per copy than either. Compare quotes.

## What about printing locally?

Print-on-demand platforms are ideal when you don't know how many copies you'll sell. But if you already know you need several hundred copies, for a school order or a launch, a print run from a local printer can cost less per book. The trade-off is money up front and boxes to store. For the whole process, from manuscript to listing, see [How to self-publish a book, step by step](/blog/how-to-self-publish-a-book-step-by-step).
