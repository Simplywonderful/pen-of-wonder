A LinkedIn profile gets found, and then gets a message, when four things are right. The headline says what you do and who for, in words people search for. The About section reads like you talking to the reader. The Experience section shows results, not job descriptions. And the whole profile uses the words recruiters and clients type into LinkedIn's search box. Here's how to do each one.

## In short

- Your headline is the most important line on the profile. Make it specific.
- Write the About section in the first person, as if you were talking.
- Show results under each role, as you would on a strong CV.
- Use the words people search for: job titles, skills and tools.
- Add a clear photo and a background banner that says something about your work.

## What makes a LinkedIn headline work?

Your headline appears with your name almost everywhere on LinkedIn: in search results, comments, connection requests and messages. It's often the only thing people read before deciding whether to click.

A good headline answers three questions: what do you do, who do you do it for, and what's different about how you do it?

| Weak | Stronger |
| --- | --- |
| Passionate professional open to opportunities | Finance officer for NGOs: budgets, donor reports and audits |
| Entrepreneur, Visionary, Leader | I help small bakeries sell more online with simple Instagram ads |
| Student at University of Lagos | Final-year economics student, interested in data analysis and public policy |

Use plain job titles and skills that people would search for. LinkedIn shows a character counter as you type, so you can see how much room you have.

## How do you write the About section?

Write it in the first person, as if you were explaining your work to someone you'd just met. People who read your About section are already interested. Give them a reason to get in touch.

A simple structure that works:

1. **Who you help and how**, in one or two sentences.
2. **What you've done**, with two or three examples or results.
3. **How you work**, or what you care about in your field.
4. **What you want next**, and how to contact you.

Keep paragraphs short. Most people read LinkedIn on their phones, and long blocks of text are hard to read on a small screen.

## How should the Experience section differ from your CV?

Your profile and your [CV](/blog/how-to-write-a-cv-that-gets-interviews) should agree on the facts: job titles, employers and dates. If they don't match, recruiters notice.

But your profile doesn't have to be written for one job. Under each role:

- Write one line on what the organisation does, if it isn't well known.
- Show two to four results, as you would on a strong CV.
- Add media where you can: a report you wrote, a presentation, a website you built, a news story about your project.

## Which words do recruiters and clients search for?

People search LinkedIn the way they search anywhere: with the plain words for what they need. A recruiter types a job title, a skill and a location. A client types the service they want.

To find your words:

1. Look at three or four job adverts, or client requests, for the work you want next.
2. Note the job titles, skills and tools that come up again and again.
3. Use those words in your headline, About section, Experience and Skills, wherever they're true for you.

If people in your field call it "monitoring and evaluation", don't call it "impact tracking". Use the words your readers use.

## What else helps a profile get noticed?

- **A clear photo.** Your face, good light, a plain background, dressed as you would be for work.
- **A background banner.** Use it to say what you do, or show your work. A plain colour is better than a random stock image.
- **A custom profile address.** LinkedIn lets you edit your public profile URL. Use your name, so it's tidy on a CV.
- **Your location and industry.** Recruiters often filter by both.
- **Recent activity.** Posting or commenting now and then shows your profile belongs to someone who's active.

## A profile check you can do in ten minutes

- [ ] Your headline says what you do and who for, in searchable words.
- [ ] Your About section is in the first person, with at least one result.
- [ ] Each recent role shows results, not just duties.
- [ ] Job titles and dates match your CV.
- [ ] Your photo is clear and recent.
- [ ] Your banner says something about your work.
- [ ] Your profile URL uses your name.

A profile that passes all seven won't guarantee messages. It will make sure that when someone looks, they understand what you do and how to reach you.
