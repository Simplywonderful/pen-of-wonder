An applicant tracking system, or ATS, is the software many employers use to collect, store and sort job applications. Recruiters use it to search for candidates, often with words taken straight from the job advert. To come out near the top, your CV needs a plain layout the system can read, standard section headings, and the advert's own words wherever they honestly describe you.

## In short

- An ATS mostly stores, sorts and searches applications. A person still makes the decision.
- The risk isn't a robot rejecting you. It's ranking low because the system can't read your CV or can't match its words.
- Use one column, standard headings and no graphics for important information.
- Mirror the advert's words for skills and job titles that are true for you.
- Send the file type the advert asks for.

## What does an applicant tracking system actually do?

An applicant tracking system does four things for a busy recruiter:

1. **Collects** every application in one place, from the company's website and job boards.
2. **Reads** each CV and pulls out details such as your name, job titles, employers, dates, education and skills.
3. **Lets the recruiter search and filter**, for example "project manager" plus "Prince2" plus a location.
4. **Tracks** each candidate through the process, from applied to interviewed to hired.

Some systems also ask screening questions when you apply, such as whether you have the right to work in that country or hold a particular licence. A "no" to an essential question can take an application out of the running. That's worth knowing, and it's another reason to read the advert carefully.

## Is it true that most CVs are rejected by a robot?

You'll often read that a large share of CVs are rejected automatically before a person sees them. I haven't found a reliable source behind those figures, so I don't repeat them.

What's real is quieter and more useful to know. If your CV is hard for the system to read, your details may land in the wrong fields or go missing. And if your CV uses different words from the advert, it may sit low in the recruiter's search results. Either way, a person may never scroll far enough to find you.

## How do you make your CV easy for an ATS to read?

Keep the layout simple. The things that make a CV look striking are often the things that confuse the software.

| Do | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| One column, top to bottom | Two or three columns side by side |
| Standard headings: Profile, Skills, Work history, Education | Creative headings such as "My journey" or "What I bring" |
| Plain text for your name and contact details | Contact details in the page header or footer |
| Simple bullet points | Tables, text boxes, icons and skill bars |
| A common font at size 11 or bigger | Unusual fonts or very small text |
| Dates in one consistent format, such as "March 2023 to May 2025" | Dates mixed across formats or missing |

A good test: copy all the text from your CV and paste it into a plain text document. If it comes out in a sensible order with nothing missing, a tracking system will probably read it well too.

## How do you match your CV to the job advert?

Recruiters search the system using words from the job advert, so your CV should use those words where they're true for you.

1. Read the advert and list the skills, tools, qualifications and job titles it mentions.
2. For each one you genuinely have, check your CV uses the same words. If the advert says "budget management" and your CV says "handled finances", change yours.
3. Use both the full term and the short form for important ones, such as "search engine optimisation (SEO)".
4. Put the most important matches in your profile and skills section, and show them in action in your work history.

Never add a skill you don't have to beat the system. A person reads your CV next, and an interviewer after that.

## Which file type should you send?

Send whatever the advert or application form asks for. If it doesn't say, a PDF keeps your layout fixed and is widely accepted. Some older systems read Word files more reliably, so if a site offers both and recommends Word, use Word.

Name the file clearly, with your name and "CV", so a recruiter can find it later.

## A quick ATS check before you apply

- [ ] One column, no tables, text boxes or graphics for key information.
- [ ] Standard section headings.
- [ ] Name and contact details in the main body, not the header.
- [ ] The advert's key words appear in your profile, skills and work history, where they're true.
- [ ] Dates are consistent and complete.
- [ ] The text pastes cleanly into a plain text document.
- [ ] The file type matches what the advert asks for.

None of this replaces a CV that shows real results; [How to write a CV that gets interviews](/blog/how-to-write-a-cv-that-gets-interviews) covers that part. It just makes sure the right person gets to read it.
