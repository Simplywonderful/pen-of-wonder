A cover letter that gets read is short, specific and written for one job. The UK National Careers Service says it's "usually 3 to 5 paragraphs long". In that space you say which role you're applying for and why this employer, show two or three pieces of evidence that you can do the job, and close by asking for the next step. Anything that only repeats your CV can go.

## In short

- Write a new letter for every application. Change more than the company name.
- Address a real person by name where you can.
- Lead with the role and one strong reason you fit it.
- Prove two or three claims with examples, not adjectives.
- Keep it to three to five paragraphs.

## What is a cover letter for?

The National Careers Service describes a cover letter as something that "introduces you to an employer and asks them to think about your application". Your CV lists what you've done. The letter connects it to this job, this employer and this moment. (If your CV needs work first, start with [How to write a CV that gets interviews](/blog/how-to-write-a-cv-that-gets-interviews).)

That's why a general letter that could go to any company does so little. It answers a question nobody asked.

## How should you structure a cover letter?

Use the same five-part shape every time, and change what goes inside it.

| Part | What it does | Length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Greeting | Addresses a named person where possible | One line |
| Opening | Names the role, where you saw it, and your strongest reason for fitting it | Two or three sentences |
| Evidence | Two or three examples that match what the advert asks for | One or two short paragraphs |
| Why them | Shows you know what the employer does and why you want to work there | Two or three sentences |
| Close | Thanks them, points to your CV, asks for an interview | Two sentences |

## How do you open a cover letter?

Skip "I am writing to apply for...". Say which role it is, and then give the reader a reason to keep going.

**Weak:** "I am writing to express my interest in the position of Marketing Assistant advertised on your website."

**Stronger:** "I'd like to apply for the Marketing Assistant role advertised on your website. For the last two years I've run the social media and email newsletter for a busy independent bookshop, and your focus on community events is exactly the kind of marketing I most enjoy."

The second opening names the role, shows relevant experience and makes a link to the employer, in three sentences.

## How do you show you're right for the job?

Pick the two or three things the advert seems to care about most, and give one piece of evidence for each. The National Careers Service suggests backing up what you say "with facts" and using the STAR method: Situation, Task, Action, Result.

Here's STAR in a single sentence or two:

> When our team's complaint backlog reached two weeks (situation), I was asked to clear it (task). I set up a daily triage list and a set of standard replies for the five most common issues (action), and within a month we were answering every complaint within three days (result).

One example like that is worth more than a paragraph of "I am hard-working, organised and a great team player".

## How do you show you know the employer?

Spend ten minutes on their website and recent news. The National Careers Service suggests researching through "their website", "recent news articles" and people you know who work there. Then write two or three sentences that show you understand what they do and why you want to do it with them.

Be specific. "I admire your organisation's values" could be about anyone. "Your move into supplying schools last year caught my attention, because I spent three years in school procurement" could only be about them.

## How should you end it?

Thank them, point them to your CV for the detail, and say you'd welcome the chance to talk. If you addressed a named person, end with "Yours sincerely". If you wrote "Dear Sir or Madam", end with "Yours faithfully".

Check your contact details are the same on the letter and the CV.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Sending the same letter everywhere.** Recruiters can tell. Write a new one each time, as the National Careers Service advises.
- **Getting the company or person's name wrong.** Check it twice. It's the fastest way to the reject pile.
- **Repeating the CV.** The letter should explain and connect, not list.
- **Writing about what you want from them.** A line about why you want the role is good. A paragraph about your career goals is too much.
- **Going long.** If it's longer than five paragraphs, cut.

## Before you send it

- [ ] The role and the company are named correctly.
- [ ] It's addressed to a person where possible.
- [ ] There are two or three concrete examples that match the advert.
- [ ] There's at least one sentence that could only be about this employer.
- [ ] It's three to five paragraphs.
- [ ] The font and size match your CV.
- [ ] You've read it aloud and checked the spelling.
