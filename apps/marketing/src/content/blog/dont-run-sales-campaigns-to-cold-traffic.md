## The objective everyone reaches for first

When most business owners open Meta Ads Manager for the first time and see the list of campaign objectives, they reach for the same one: Sales. That's the goal, after all: why would you run an ad with any other objective?

Because the people seeing your ad have never heard of you.

## Cold traffic isn't ready to buy

Cold audiences on Meta aren't ready to buy. They scrolled past your ad while looking at something else entirely. They don't know your brand, they haven't seen your offer, and they have no reason to hand over their money to a stranger. Running a sales campaign to cold traffic is asking someone to marry you before you've introduced yourself. The objective makes sense. The timing doesn't.

## The three objectives that actually work early on

For a business just starting to run ads, three campaign objectives are worth using:

1. **Leads.** Collect contact information before asking anyone to spend money. Once someone's on your list, you follow up and build trust on your own terms.
2. **Traffic.** Sends people to your content or landing page: how you warm people up at scale. Someone who's visited your site is no longer cold.
3. **Engagement.** Builds familiarity. Likes, comments, and shares are signals people recognize and respond to: an engaged audience is easier to sell to later.

**Leads** is the most important. A leads campaign is designed to collect contact information before you ask anyone to spend money. Once someone is on your list, you can follow up, build trust, and make the sale through a process that doesn't depend on a split-second decision from someone who just discovered you exist. You own that contact: the ad spend that brought them in already did its job.

**Traffic** sends people to your content, your landing page, or wherever you need them to go. It's how you warm people up at scale. Someone who has visited your website is no longer cold. Someone who has read your content knows something about you. Traffic campaigns build the audience your future campaigns will actually be able to convert.

**Engagement** builds familiarity. Likes, comments, and shares are signals people recognize and respond to. An audience that has engaged with your content is easier to sell to later, because you're no longer a stranger.

## Where the sales objective actually belongs

The sales objective has its place, but that place is retargeting. When someone has already been through your lead funnel, visited your website, or is on your email list, running a sales campaign to that warm audience makes sense. They know you. The gap between seeing the ad and buying is much shorter.

For a new business running ads for the first time: start with leads, build the list, warm the audience, then sell to people who already know why they should say yes.
