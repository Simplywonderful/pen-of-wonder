## Not everything I teach came from a course

Not everything I teach here came from a course. This one didn't. I learned it by actually working on ads, watching what happens when everything looks right on paper and the numbers still don't move.

## Diagnosis starts with numbers, not a feeling

Diagnosing a weak campaign starts with the numbers, not a feeling: cost per click, cost per message, ROAS, whatever your specific objective is priced against. A weak campaign is one costing more than it should to hit its goal, and that verdict only counts once [the campaign has actually cleared the learning phase](/blog/why-most-ad-budgets-actually-get-wasted). Judging it before that is judging noise.

## The situation most people skip past

Here's the situation that actually needs real diagnosis, and it's the one most people skip past. Your ad copy is proven to convert. Your creative is a genuine high performer. Your targeting is reaching the right audience. All three are in place, and the campaign is still underperforming on cost per result. When that happens, something is wrong somewhere else, and throwing more budget at it doesn't fix a problem you haven't located yet.

## Where to look first: is the offer actually competitive?

The first place I look is the offer itself. Not whether it's good, whether it's competitive. An offer can be genuinely solid and still lose, because somewhere in that same market, a competitor is offering something that looks better on the surface to the exact same buyer. Good is not the bar. Better than what else they're seeing is the bar.

## Where to look second: is the tracking measuring the real thing?

The second place I look is tracking. Meta can only optimize toward the signal you told it to chase, and if that signal is wrong, everything downstream is compromised even while the campaign looks structurally perfect. A common mistake here is [tracking a button click instead of an actual completed action](/blog/sales-campaigns-vs-leads-campaigns-meta-pixel-tracking): someone landing on a real thank-you page, an actual form submission registering. If the algorithm is learning from a false signal, it'll confidently keep sending you the wrong kind of person, and no amount of extra budget corrects a lesson built on bad data.

These aren't the only reasons a campaign underperforms with everything seemingly in place. They're the two I've run into and had to work through myself. There will be others I haven't hit yet.
