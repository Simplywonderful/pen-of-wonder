## Showing up, repeatedly, is only half the job

In business, you have to keep showing up in front of people. Ads are one of the most reliable ways to do that: people need to keep seeing you and hearing about what you sell, over and over, before most of them decide anything.

What's less obvious is that there seem to be three specific ways ad copy pushes someone toward an actual buying decision, not just attention.

1. **Pain.** Keep bringing the prospect back to the problem, without softening it: the clearer the cost of inaction feels, the more urgent solving it becomes.
2. **Others' results.** Showing people you've already helped does more convincing than claims about yourself, because someone else is living the outcome.
3. **Your own results.** Your own dashboard, your own numbers: proof you're a practitioner of what you're selling, not just a teacher of it.

## Pain: uncomfortable, but it holds up

The first is pain. You keep bringing the prospect back to the problem, again and again, without softening it. This one can feel close to overdoing it at first, but the logic holds up once you sit with it: the more clearly someone feels the cost of not solving their problem, the more convinced they become that solving it is worth acting on now, not later.

## Other people's results: the bandwagon effect

The second is showing the results of people you've already helped. This does more convincing than almost anything you could say about yourself directly, because it's not you making the claim. It's someone else living the outcome. When people see others getting results, they start picturing themselves getting the same result. That's the bandwagon effect at work.

This is likely where UGC-style content earns its reputation. It works specifically because it doesn't look or sound like an ad. It looks like someone talking to a friend about something that worked for them, which is different from a polished testimonial video, even though both are technically showing results.

## Your own results: proof you practice what you teach

The third is showing your own results: your own dashboard, your own before-and-after, your own revenue numbers where you can share them. This tells people you're not just teaching a theory, you're a practitioner of what you're selling.

## None of these replace the others

Pain gets attention. Other people's results build belief. Your own results build trust in you specifically. How these three stack in a single piece of ad copy versus across a sequence of ads is worth testing directly: run the same offer in two ad sets, one leading with pain, one leading with someone else's result, same audience, same budget: whichever wins tells you which lever your specific audience responds to first.
