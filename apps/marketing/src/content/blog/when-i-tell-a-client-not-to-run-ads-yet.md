## Leading with questions, not a pitch

A few days ago, someone reached out about running ads for their book sales. By the end of the conversation, I told them ads was not what they needed right now.

I didn't lead with a pitch. I led with questions. What industry, what audience, what platform, how engaged is that audience, what's the ad budget, what does the ideal buyer actually look like, how responsive have people been so far. Then the one that mattered most: what has revenue looked like over the last three months, and is that number documented anywhere reliable.

## What the answers actually revealed

They sell manuscripts as unpublished e-books, priced from ₦20,000, promoted through direct outreach to an existing LinkedIn audience. Revenue over the last three months was ₦500,000. Sounds solid, until the next two questions: was that number documented beyond a personal summary, and was it net profit, or outright sales revenue with no expenditure removed from it.

It was outright revenue. Undocumented beyond a personal log. Sold randomly, not consistently.

That told me everything I needed to know. Running ads on top of a sales process that's not consistent or properly tracked doesn't fix the inconsistency. It just [adds paid traffic on top of a leak nobody has found yet](/blog/three-questions-before-i-run-your-ads). I told them plainly that the priority was fixing the sales strategy first, and that ads could come after, once we understood what was actually working and what wasn't.

## Why this part of the work gets skipped

This is the part of the work most ads people skip, because it's easier to just take the budget and run the campaign. I'd rather tell someone the truth and lose the sale today than take their money for a service that won't fix their actual problem.

This is who I work best with: not someone looking for ads to rescue a business problem ads can't solve, but someone willing to answer uncomfortable questions honestly, even when the answer isn't flattering, because they actually want the number to change. Not just the appearance of doing something about it.
