## The assumption that kills campaigns before launch

Having something to sell and knowing who to sell it to are two completely different skills, and most people never separate them.

Here's the assumption that quietly kills a lot of campaigns before they even launch: treating "everybody" as the target audience. If your goal is actual sales and profit, not charity, everybody was never the plan. You have to first accept that your audience is a specific slice of the population, not the whole thing.

## Start with age

Meta lets you target people as young as 18, and most advertisers just leave the range wide open because it's there. But look at who's actually in that 18 to 22 bracket: mostly students, people just finishing school, or people just starting out. Most of them aren't earning their own money yet, and whatever they spend is usually someone else's.

If your product needs someone with real earning capacity, that age range isn't your audience just because Meta lets you select it. Depending on what you're selling, a more realistic floor is often 23 and above.

## Two ways to target people with money, not just interest

There are two ways I know of to target people who actually have the money to buy, not just the interest. This post covers the first, location. The second is [interest-based targeting combined with Engaged Shoppers](/blog/target-affluent-buyers-with-interests-and-engaged-shoppers).

## Location: pick the states, then the neighbourhoods

Say I'm selling luxury bags to women in Nigeria. I'd start by setting the audience to women, then find out which states in the country actually have money concentrated in them. A quick search gets you somewhere: Lagos, Rivers, and Akwa Ibom, for instance.

1. **Set the audience.** Women, for a luxury bag example, with an age floor that reflects real earning capacity.
2. **Pick the states.** Find which states have money concentrated in them, for example Lagos, Rivers and Akwa Ibom.
3. **Go a level deeper.** Find the specific areas inside each state where the wealth actually sits, and stack them all into your location targeting.

In Lagos, that means areas like Banana Island, Ikoyi, Eko Atlantic, Victoria Island, Lekki Phase 1, Nicon Town, Ikeja GRA, Magodo, and VGC. You do the same for the other states and stack all of it into your location targeting.

## A note on that neighbourhood list

I pulled those areas from a general search, not a verified income dataset. Treat it as a starting point for research, not a definitive ranking of the richest areas. Always validate against your own audience insights and ad performance once you're running.

## Why a big audience number is fine here

At this point your audience might still show 3 to 5 million people or more, and that's fine. I'm not trying to shrink the number yet; that comes from other targeting layers, like [stacking interests, behaviours and demographics](/blog/interests-behaviors-demographics-meta-targeting-layers). Location alone just gets you into the right rooms.

## What location can't do

One thing this doesn't replace: you still need to know exactly who you're selling to before any of this matters. Rich people in the right location will still scroll past you if your copy and creative don't sound like they're speaking to them specifically. Location gets your ad in front of people with money. It doesn't make your message land.
