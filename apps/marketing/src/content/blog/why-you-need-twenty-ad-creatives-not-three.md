## Performance stalls before it starts

Most people run one or two ad creatives and wonder why performance stalls before it ever really starts. The problem isn't usually the offer. It's that they never gave the algorithm anything to actually learn from.

The baseline most people know is to have at least three creatives before you launch anything, ideally video. But if you're asking me for a professional recommendation, three isn't enough. I'd start with at least twenty.

## Why twenty, specifically

The reason is simple. The algorithm needs enough options to actually find out what works. Twenty creatives give it room to test properly, instead of you guessing which single video will land. Once the testing phase is done, you scale only what actually performed, with real data behind the decision instead of a hunch.

1. **Fear.** Four variations built around what's at risk if the problem stays unsolved.
2. **Pain.** Four variations that sit with the current cost of the problem.
3. **Pleasure.** Four variations built around the upside of solving it.
4. **Pride / accomplishment.** Four variations built around identity and achievement.
5. **Results.** Four variations built around concrete, provable outcomes.

Twenty sounds like a lot until you break it down: five angles, four videos each, built around different levers such as urgency, scarcity, testimonials, or a guarantee. That's your twenty. Not twenty random videos, twenty deliberate variations testing which emotional angle and which lever actually moves your specific audience.

## What the algorithm does with all twenty

The algorithm does the rest. It shows all twenty to your audience and starts figuring out which angle and which lever resonates with the people you're actually trying to reach. You're not guessing anymore. You're watching the data tell you.

## The second benefit: buying runway against ad fatigue

There's a second benefit people don't think about until it bites them: ad fatigue. When you're only running one or two creatives, your audience sees the same thing over and over until they stop noticing it. They scroll past without registering it's even an ad anymore. Twenty creatives, rotated properly, buys you real runway before fatigue sets in.

The testing doesn't stop once you find a winner, either. Once you know which angle performs best, you build more videos inside that same angle, testing new hooks, new calls to action, sometimes even new offers. You're not done after round one. You're compounding on what you now know works.

Most people treat creative as the thing they throw together last, right before launch. It should be the opposite. Creative volume is not decoration around a campaign. It's the actual testing engine. If it's thin, [no targeting fix or budget increase underneath it will save the campaign](/blog/why-most-ad-budgets-actually-get-wasted).
