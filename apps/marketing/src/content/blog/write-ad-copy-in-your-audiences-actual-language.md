## Most ad copy fails before the pitch even starts

Most ad copy fails before the offer is even mentioned. It fails at the first line, because it doesn't sound like something a real person would say to another real person.

One approach to this starts in an unusual place for a copywriting lesson. Not with headlines, not with formulas, but with research. Before you write a single line of copy, you need to know the actual language your audience uses to describe their own problem.

## Where to actually find that language

That means finding where they gather: groups, forums, comment sections, whatever platform your specific audience actually lives on. Once you're there, you're not reading for ideas. You're reading for exact words. How do they describe what's going wrong for them? What phrases do they reach for? What do they call the thing that's frustrating them?

A practical starting point: pull actual quotes, not paraphrases, from where your audience talks. Paraphrasing is where you accidentally slip back into business language without noticing.

## Why this matters more than any formula

This matters because the gap between how a business talks about a problem and how a customer actually experiences it is usually wide. Businesses use industry language. Customers use plain, specific, sometimes messy language. If your copy sounds like the business, it flies straight over the head of the person you're trying to reach. They read it, feel nothing, and scroll on. Your ad spend just paid for that scroll.

When you write using the exact language your audience already uses to describe their problem, something different happens. They read it and think: this person understands what I'm going through. That recognition does something no clever headline formula can do on its own. It builds trust before you've said a single word about your offer. Once someone feels understood, they're far more willing to listen to what you're proposing as the solution, and far more willing to believe you might actually have it.

This is the part most people skip. They want the clever hook, the punchy CTA, the formula. None of that works if the words underneath it don't sound like something their audience would actually say. Which is also why the [strongest hooks](/blog/the-hook-you-think-will-work-probably-wont) borrow real language, not a marketer's version of it.

Research the language first. Copy that sounds human is usually just copy that borrowed its words honestly.
