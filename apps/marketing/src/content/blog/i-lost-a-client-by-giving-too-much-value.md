## It sounds backwards, but it happened

I lost a client once by giving them too much value. That sounds backwards, but it's exactly what happened.

They reached out wanting to boost a post. That was it. Simple ask. Instead of answering that, I went into a full explanation of [why boosting wasn't the same as running a real campaign](/blog/boosting-vs-running-a-real-meta-campaign), what a proper lead generation funnel would look like, what email marketing setup they'd need, and everything else I believed they needed to actually succeed.

I wasn't wrong about any of it. I was just answering a question nobody asked me.

They said they'd think about it. I didn't hear from them again for months.

## What I actually got wrong

What I got wrong wasn't the information. It was the order. I never asked what they actually wanted or why they wanted it. I just started dispensing what I considered valuable, before I understood whether that value was even relevant to where they were. They got overwhelmed, not convinced.

## The second time round

Months later, that same person referred me to a colleague who wanted to do the exact same thing: boost a post. Nothing more. This time I didn't repeat the mistake. I asked what they wanted, confirmed that was genuinely what they wanted and not just what they thought ads meant, and then I gave them exactly that.

I'll be honest: I wasn't fully satisfied with the results. I could see what more was possible if the setup around it had been stronger. But that wasn't the conversation they'd asked for, and pushing it on them again would have cost me the same way it did the first time.

## The part that stuck with me

The referral is the part that stuck with me most. Even the client who left unhappy with how much information I threw at them still trusted me enough to send someone else my way. That told me the value wasn't wrong. The timing was.

## What I've learned

If someone is open to hearing more, that door has to be opened by them, or at least earned, not forced. Give people what they actually asked for first. If they're curious about more, they'll ask, or they'll let you offer it. My job is to understand what they actually want before I decide what they need. It's also why my [conversation order with new clients](/blog/the-order-i-qualify-a-new-ads-client) starts with questions, not a pitch.
