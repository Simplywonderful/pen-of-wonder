A good cost per lead is one your business can afford, and nobody else's average can tell you what that is. You can work it out with three numbers you already have: what a new client is worth to you, how many leads it takes to win one, and how much of that value you're willing to spend on winning them. The rest of this post shows how.

## In short

- Your maximum cost per lead = profit from a new client × your close rate.
- Industry benchmarks mix very different businesses, so treat them with care.
- A cheap lead that never buys is more expensive than a costly one that does.
- Judge your ads on cost per client, not only cost per lead.

## Why other people's benchmarks mislead you

Search "average cost per lead" and you'll find tables by industry. They're tempting, because they give you a number to aim at. But they mix together businesses with different prices, offers, countries and sales processes.

A coach who sells a 3,000 programme can afford to pay far more for a lead than a shop selling a 30 product. Both could be "in coaching" or "in retail" in someone's table. The only target that fits your business is one built from your own numbers.

## The three numbers you need

**1. What a new client is worth to you.** Use profit, not price. If you sell a course for 200 and it costs you 50 to deliver, a client is worth 150. If clients usually buy more than once, you can use the profit from what a typical client buys over a year, but be honest about it.

**2. Your close rate.** This is the share of leads who become paying clients. If 40 people contacted you last month and 4 paid, your close rate is 4 ÷ 40 = 10%. If you've never tracked it, start now; even a month of records is better than a guess.

**3. How much you're willing to spend to win a client.** Spending all of a client's profit on winning them leaves nothing for you. Most businesses decide on a share, such as a third or a half, depending on how much they value growth now.

## The calculation, worked through

Here's the formula:

**Maximum cost per lead = profit per client × close rate**

And your **target** cost per lead is that number multiplied by the share you're willing to spend.

| Step | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Profit from a new client | 150 |
| Close rate | 10% (1 in 10 leads pays) |
| Maximum cost per lead (break-even) | 150 × 10% = 15 |
| Share of profit you'll spend to win a client | 50% |
| Target cost per lead | 15 × 50% = 7.50 |

So in this example, leads under 7.50 are comfortably profitable, leads between 7.50 and 15 are worth it if you want growth, and leads over 15 lose money on the first sale.

The numbers work the same in any currency. Use your own.

## Why cost per client matters more

Cost per lead tells you what you paid for a contact. Cost per client tells you what you paid for a sale. The second is the one that pays your bills.

Two campaigns can tell very different stories:

| | Campaign A | Campaign B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spend | 300 | 300 |
| Leads | 60 | 20 |
| Cost per lead | 5 | 15 |
| Clients | 2 | 5 |
| Cost per client | 150 | 60 |

Campaign A looks better on cost per lead. Campaign B is the one that worked. If you only watch cost per lead, you'll switch off the wrong campaign.

## What if you don't know your close rate yet?

Then the first campaign's job is to find out. Keep the budget modest, record every lead and what happened to it, and calculate your close rate at the end of the month. That number is worth more than any benchmark.

Two things to record for every lead:

- which ad or campaign it came from
- whether it became a paying client, and for how much

That's enough to work out both cost per lead and cost per client for every campaign you run. [Ad tracking for small businesses](/blog/ad-tracking-pixel-conversions-api-and-utms) shows how to record the first one automatically.

## How to lower your cost per lead without lowering lead quality

If your cost per lead is above your target, check these before cutting the budget:

1. **Is the offer clear?** Vague ads get clicks from the curious and leads from no one.
2. **Is the landing page or form doing its job?** Many "expensive lead" problems are really page problems. See [Why your ads get clicks but no leads](/blog/why-your-ads-get-clicks-but-no-leads).
3. **Are you testing enough creative?** One ad gives the system nothing to compare.
4. **Are you replying fast?** A faster reply doesn't change cost per lead, but it raises your close rate, which raises what each lead is worth.

The last one is the cheapest fix of all. A higher close rate makes every lead you've already paid for more valuable.
