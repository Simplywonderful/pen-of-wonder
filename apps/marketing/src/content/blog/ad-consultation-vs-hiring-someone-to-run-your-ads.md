## What is an ad consultation?

An ad consultation is a conversation where someone looks at what you're seeing in your ads and tells you what it means. It's different from hiring someone to run the campaigns for you.

A recent example: [a new advertiser asked for an ad expert and turned out to need someone to talk through their campaign](/blog/why-your-first-meta-ads-campaign-isnt-getting-sign-ups), not someone to take it over.

## Ad consultation vs ad management: what's the difference?

| | Ad consultation | Ad management |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What you get | Someone looks at what you're seeing and tells you what it means | Someone runs the ads for you |
| Who it suits | Business owners, and the person at a company who has been handed the ads and told to figure it out | Businesses that need someone to run the ads |
| How I start | A free first conversation | A conversation first, before any pricing |

If you're not sure which you need, that's what the first conversation is for.

## How does an ad consultation work with me?

The first conversation is free. If you want more after that, the next ones are paid, based on how long we spend. What we cover depends on what your business needs.

Before I suggest anything, I ask a series of questions about your business and what you're trying to do. That's the same approach I describe in [what happens when you message me about ads](/blog/the-order-i-qualify-a-new-ads-client): conversation first, offer second.

## What problems does a consultation usually find?

Sometimes the problem is how the campaign is set up. Sometimes it's which numbers someone is watching. I won't know until I ask.

## Who is it for?

It's for business owners and for the person at a company who has been handed the ads and told to figure it out. In the recent example above, the person wanted to figure it out themselves, so I gave direction and didn't take over. If you need someone to run the ads, we can talk about that too, and [how I run a campaign](/blog/how-i-actually-run-a-campaign) is laid out in detail. It also helps to know [who I work best with](/blog/who-i-work-best-with), and that I'll sometimes say [not to run ads yet](/blog/when-i-tell-a-client-not-to-run-ads-yet) if the groundwork isn't there.
