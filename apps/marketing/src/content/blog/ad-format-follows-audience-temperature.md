## Format is a strategy decision, not a design one

The format your ad runs in is not a design decision. It's a strategy decision.

Most people choose between image, video, and carousel based on what looks good or what they have available. That's the wrong starting point. The right starting point is the temperature of your audience.

## Why cold audiences need video

Cold audiences have never heard of you. They don't know your brand, your offer, or why they should care. Showing them a static image or a carousel and expecting them to make a decision is asking too much too fast. These people need to feel like they're dealing with a real person before they'll do anything. A well-made video solves that problem.

Within the first few seconds, the viewer is either engaged or not. If the hook lands, you've earned a few more seconds to state your offer, address their hesitation, flip the risk in their favor, and walk them toward a decision: all inside roughly ninety seconds, because that's about the limit of attention you have before most people are gone.

> **~90 seconds**: Roughly the attention window for a cold-audience video ad. Every second beyond what's necessary is a second where you're losing people

Ninety seconds sounds short. It's enough, if you're disciplined about what goes into it. Every second that doesn't move the viewer closer to a decision is a second wasted.

## Where image and carousel formats actually work

Image and carousel formats have their place, but that place is specific. They work when the offer is low-ticket enough that the buyer doesn't need much convincing. They work when the product or service is so clearly in demand that a strong visual and a sharp line of copy is all it takes. They work when you've already tested the creative and the data has confirmed it converts. They work for engagement and awareness campaigns where the goal is familiarity, not an immediate decision.

What they don't do well is replace the trust-building that video handles for cold audiences who've never encountered your brand before.

Format follows function. Decide who you're talking to and what you need them to do: the right format becomes obvious from there.
