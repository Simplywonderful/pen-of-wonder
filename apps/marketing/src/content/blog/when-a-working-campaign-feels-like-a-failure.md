## A campaign that worked, and still felt like a failure

I want to tell you about a campaign that worked, and still felt like a failure to the person who paid for it.

By every metric I could see in Meta's Events Manager (cost per click, number of messages generated) the campaign was doing what it was supposed to do at that stage. It was still in its [learning phase](/blog/why-most-ad-budgets-actually-get-wasted), still finding its audience. That's normal. That's expected.

The client didn't see it that way. They felt they were spending too much for too little, and honestly, what they were spending wasn't even enough to properly train the algorithm on the right customer fit for their product. Two very different readings of the same numbers.

## Why the pressure felt different this time

Here's the context that mattered: this was a starting business. The ad spend was coming out of the client's personal funds, not business income, because the business hadn't started generating revenue yet. Every naira spent felt heavier to them than it would to someone spending out of existing profit. I understood that. I still understand it.

But understanding it put me under pressure to do things I know better than to do. I retweaked the targeted audience mid-flight. I readjusted the daily ad spend more than once. I made changes I'd tell any client not to make, because every change resets part of what the algorithm has already learned. I was trying to perform magic instead of trusting the process I teach other people to trust.

## What I actually got wrong

That's on me, not on the client. They were reacting to real fear about real money. My job was to hold the line and explain clearly what the learning phase requires. Not to cave to the pressure and start moving levers just to be seen doing something.

## What changed in how I take on clients now

It taught me something I now build into every first conversation. I ask what your expectation is going in, and what success actually looks like to you, specifically. Not what I think success looks like, what you do. Because if we don't agree on that before the campaign starts, we'll disagree about the results no matter how good they are.

Alongside [the budget question, the ideal buyer question, and the revenue range question](/blog/when-i-tell-a-client-not-to-run-ads-yet), those two, expectation and definition of success, are now non-negotiable before I take on any client. Not because I'm trying to be difficult. Because I'd rather lose a client at the questions stage than lose their trust three weeks into a campaign that's actually working.
