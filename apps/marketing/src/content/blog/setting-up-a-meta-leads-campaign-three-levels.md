## Recording the setup, not the highlight reel

I set up a leads campaign this week and recorded every step. Not a sanitized walkthrough, the actual campaign I was running, with every section labeled so you could follow exactly what I was doing and why.

The setup moves through three levels: campaign, ad set, ad. Each level has one job. When you understand the job, the decisions become obvious. When you don't, you make expensive mistakes in places you didn't realize were costly.

1. **Campaign level.** Select the Leads objective, name the campaign, and set the budget: including whether budget lives at the campaign or the ad set.
2. **Ad set level.** Set the conversion location, connect the dataset for tracking, and select from saved audiences.
3. **Ad level.** Attach the instant form, pick the video creative, add several variations of copy and headlines, and let Meta test them.

## What actually happens at each level

At the campaign level, I select the Leads objective, name the campaign, and set the budget. The choice between campaign-level budget and ad set budget matters more than most people realize, especially during the testing phase when the algorithm is still figuring out what works.

At the ad set level, I set the conversion location, connect my dataset for tracking, and select from my saved audiences. The tracking step is where most people fall short: if it isn't configured before you spend anything, [Meta can show you numbers that look fine while the actual results tell a different story](/blog/sales-campaigns-vs-leads-campaigns-meta-pixel-tracking).

At the ad level, I attach the instant form, pick the video creative, add five variations of ad copy and headlines, and let Meta test them. Then I publish.

> **Under 3 minutes**: From selecting the objective to hitting publish, once the tracking and audiences were already in place

## Why the tracking step is the one to get right

The step most people ask about after watching a walkthrough like this is the dataset and tracking setup. If it's skipped, that campaign's data can't be fully trusted: the numbers might look fine on the surface while telling you nothing reliable about what's actually working.
