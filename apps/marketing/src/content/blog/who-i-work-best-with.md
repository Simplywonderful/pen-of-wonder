## Learning who's actually ready, the hard way

Not every business that wants to run ads is ready to run ads well. I've learned that the hard way, and it's shaped how I think about who I take on.

## They already know ads are the next step, not a question mark

The businesses I work best with aren't beginners trying to figure out whether ads are worth it. They already know. They've seen competitors run ads, or they've tried it themselves and hit a wall, or they simply understand that paid traffic is how you scale a proven offer beyond your existing network. They come in with a question, not a premise: not *do ads work?* but *what's wrong with what we've been doing?*

## The offer is already working

There's proof the product solves a real problem for a real person: maybe not at the scale they want yet, but someone is buying. That matters because [ads don't create demand, they reach people who already have the problem you solve](/blog/three-questions-before-i-run-your-ads). If the offer hasn't converted without ads, more traffic won't fix it.

## They commit the budget and the time

Not just for a week to see what happens: long enough for the algorithm to learn, for the creative to be tested properly, for the data to tell us something worth acting on. Pulling a campaign at day ten because nothing happened is not a failed experiment. It's an incomplete one.

## They give me room to work

Not because I need to be unchecked, but because the best campaigns come from testing methodically, reading what the data says, and adjusting based on evidence rather than instinct. I report everything. I explain every decision. But I need a client who trusts the process enough to let it run.

1. **Proven offer.** Real evidence someone has already paid for this, not just said they'd love it.
2. **Time and budget.** Committed for long enough to let the algorithm actually learn, not judged in week one.
3. **Trust in the process.** Room to test methodically and adjust on evidence, without instinct overriding the data mid-flight.
