## What I'm claiming, and what I'm not

In June I rebuilt a lead campaign, and I want to be clear about what that does and doesn't prove.

## The old campaign

The old campaign had run for about four weeks, optimised for WhatsApp conversations. It was bringing in conversations, but each one was costing far more than the business could sustain.

## The rebuilt campaign

I rebuilt it and changed three things at once: the audience, the optimisation goal (from WhatsApp conversations to website leads), and the creative.

In its first two days it brought in more leads than the old campaign had produced in weeks, at a small fraction of the old cost per result. Only a small share of those leads went on to pay.

## Now the part most people skip

A WhatsApp conversation and a website lead are not the same result. Someone who starts a chat has usually shown more intent than someone who fills in a short form. So the old cost per conversation against the new cost per lead is not a like-for-like comparison, however good the new number looks.

Because I changed three things together, I also can't tell you which change did the work. It could be the audience, the goal, the creative, or the three interacting.

## What I did learn

A rebuild is a good way to find out fast whether the old setup was the problem. Next time I'll change one thing at a time, so the result teaches me something specific. That's the same discipline behind [testing a single variable, like the targeting fix](/blog/or-logic-vs-and-logic-meta-targeting), and it's why [verifying tracking before trusting a result](/blog/diagnosing-a-campaign-that-should-be-working) matters as much as the result itself.

If you're judging your own rebuild, ask three questions before you celebrate:

1. **Am I comparing the same result?** A conversation, a lead and a sale are three different things.
2. **What did I change?** If it was more than one thing, you've learned that the old setup was worse, not why.
3. **Did the cheaper results turn into customers?** A lower cost per lead means little if fewer of them buy.
