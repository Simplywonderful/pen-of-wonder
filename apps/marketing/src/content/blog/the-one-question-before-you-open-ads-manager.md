## The question that decides everything downstream

Before you open Ads Manager, before you choose an objective, before you set a budget, there's one question that decides whether the campaign has any chance of working.

Who, specifically, is this for?

Not "business owners." Not "people who need this." A real description of a real person. What they do, what they earn, what they read, what problems they're quietly trying to solve, what they aspire to, and what would make them stop scrolling long enough to pay attention to an ad about your offer. That's your customer avatar. The targeting you build inside Meta is only as good as the clarity of that description.

## Where most businesses go wrong

This is where most businesses run into trouble. They either skip this step entirely, or they define the avatar so broadly it covers almost everyone. The instinct to serve everybody is understandable. The problem is that an ad built for everyone is an ad built for no one: the budget gets distributed across people who are mildly curious, people who'll never buy, and people who are just watching to see what happens. You burn money and collect data that tells you almost nothing useful.

## Translating the avatar into Meta's targeting

Once you have a clear customer avatar, the next challenge is translating it into Meta's targeting options. This isn't always straightforward. Not every keyword that describes your ideal customer exists inside Ads Manager. When that happens, the instinct is to pick the nearest approximation and move on.

A better approach is to go to the **Meta Ads Library**, search for ads similar to your offer, and study how those campaigns are targeted. An ad that's been running for months is a campaign that's working. The targeting behind it is worth learning from. You don't copy it blindly, but you use it as a reference to build something more informed.

One extra step worth taking: when you find an ad that's been running a long time and selling something similar to your offer, look at the comments section too. The objections and questions in those comments tell you more about the audience than any targeting keyword.

## The offer still has to hold up

Understanding the audience also includes an honest look at the offer. Before a campaign goes live, I ask whether the offer is clear enough and strong enough to actually appeal to the specific person being targeted. If the product doesn't deliver on what it promises to that person, correct targeting will only help you find the problem faster.

The businesses that waste the most money on ads aren't the ones who made wrong assumptions about their audience: wrong assumptions get corrected. An intentional business notices when the data doesn't match expectations, goes back, and refines the avatar. The businesses that waste the most are the ones who never defined the audience in the first place, and have nothing to go back and correct.
