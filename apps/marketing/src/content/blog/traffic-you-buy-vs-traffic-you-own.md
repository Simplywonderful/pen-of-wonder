## Measuring the wrong finish line

Most people measure an ad by what it produces on the platform: leads generated, link clicks, messages received, cost per result. Those numbers matter. But they're measuring the wrong finish line.

Every lead, every click, every message that comes from a paid campaign lives on a platform you don't own. Meta owns it. LinkedIn owns it. TikTok owns it. The moment that platform changes its algorithm, increases its ad costs, or restricts your account, that traffic is gone. You didn't build an asset. You rented attention.

## What every ad's real job should be

The real goal of every ad you run should be to move people off the platform and into something you control. For most Nigerian businesses, that's a WhatsApp broadcast list or a WhatsApp community. For businesses targeting international audiences, it's an email list, or increasingly, a Telegram channel. The specific platform matters less than the principle: you need somewhere that traffic goes after the ad does its job: somewhere the relationship continues without you paying again to reach the same person.

## Traffic you buy vs. traffic you own

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Rented** | Traffic you buy: stops the moment you stop paying, owned by the platform |
| **Owned** | Traffic you own: compounds, reachable again without a new ad spend |

This is the difference between traffic you buy and traffic you own. Traffic you buy stops the moment you stop paying. Traffic you own compounds. Every person who joins your list, your community, your broadcast is someone you can reach again without running another ad. They already know who you are. The next time you have an offer, a launch, or something worth saying, you're not starting from zero.

## The leak most campaigns never notice

Most ad campaigns are set up without this in mind. The ad runs, the leads come in: then what? If the answer is "we follow up manually" or "we retarget them later," that's a system with a leak. The traffic came in and went nowhere permanent.

Before the next campaign goes live, the question worth asking isn't just what objective to run or what creative to use. It's where are these people going after this, and will I still be able to reach them in six months without paying for them again? That answer changes how you build the entire campaign.
