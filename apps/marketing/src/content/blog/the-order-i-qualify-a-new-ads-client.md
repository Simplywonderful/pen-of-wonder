## Not a rate card: a conversation first

People assume that once you DM me about running ads, the next thing is a rate card. It's not. Here's what actually happens, in order, every time.

1. **Is it proven?.** What are you selling, and is it already selling? Ads on top of an unproven offer just spend money finding that out slower and more expensively.
2. **What do you expect?.** What you think you're getting, how long you think it takes, what the outcome looks like: surfaced and corrected against reality.
3. **Then, the offer.** Only once the business is proven and expectations are aligned do cost, budget, and what it gets you come up.

## Step one: has this actually sold before?

The first thing I do is not talk about ads at all. I ask about the business. What are you selling, and is it already selling? I need to know whether you have a product or service that's proven it can convert, or whether the hope is that ads will manufacture demand for something that's never sold yet. Those are two completely different conversations, and most people don't realize which one they're actually having until I ask.

If what you're selling hasn't shown it can sell to real people already, [running ads on top of it doesn't fix that](/blog/when-i-tell-a-client-not-to-run-ads-yet). It just spends money finding out the same thing slower and more expensively. I'd rather find that out in a DM than three weeks into a campaign.

## Step two: what do you actually expect?

Once I understand the business and I'm confident there's something real to sell, I move to expectations. Not mine: yours. What do you think you're getting, how long do you think it takes, what do you think the outcome looks like? I ask because most people walk in with a picture in their head that nobody gave them, built from a friend's result, a course, a screenshot they saw online, and it's rarely accurate.

That's where I do the correcting. I lay out what you're actually getting, realistically how long it takes, and what a fair outcome looks like, given everything I now know about your business. Not the best case someone sold you: the honest case, based on what's actually in front of me.

## Step three: only now, the offer

Only after all of that is settled do we talk about the offer itself. What the service costs, what you should budget, and what that gets you. If the business isn't proven and the expectations aren't aligned, we're not getting to that conversation at all.

This isn't me being difficult. It's the only order that protects both of us. I've taken shortcuts on this order before, and it never ends where either of us wants it to.
