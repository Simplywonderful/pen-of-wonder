## An assumption that sounded logical

I used to think more money on an ad meant faster results. Testing one this week corrected me.

Meta gives you two broad ways to bid: spend-based, which is the lowest cost option, and a target cost bid, where you tell the algorithm what you want to pay per result. My assumption going in was simple: bid high, give Meta room to explore, and its own intelligence would find my ideal buyer faster. It sounded logical. More budget signals more importance; more importance should mean faster learning.

That's not how it works, at least not for a new account or an unproven ad.

## What actually happens when you bid high too early

What actually happens when you bid high on a creative and copy combination that has never been tested is you're telling Meta to spend more money finding out if that combination works *at all*. You're paying a premium to test something you have no evidence for yet. The algorithm isn't smarter because you gave it more money. It's just more expensive while it learns.

Spend-based, lowest-cost bidding does something different. It tells Meta to get you the best possible results for whatever budget you set, without a ceiling on cost per result. For a business still in the testing phase, still trying to understand who its ideal buyer actually is and how to find more people like them, this is the better starting point. You're not trying to control cost yet. You're trying to learn cheaply.

## Where target cost bidding actually earns its place

Where a target cost bid earns its place is after you have a proven ad. Once a creative and copy combination has already shown it converts, that's when you start protecting your cost per result and scaling with intention. Bidding strategy isn't a fixed choice. It changes with what stage your ad is in.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Lowest cost** | New account or unproven creative: the goal is learning cheaply, not controlling cost |
| **Target cost** | Proven creative: the goal shifts to protecting cost per result while scaling |

I learned this the way I learn most things now: by testing what I assumed against what the account actually did. My assumption was reasonable. It was also wrong. The data corrected it faster than any course could have.

If you're running ads on a new account, or testing creatives that haven't proven themselves yet, start with the lowest cost bid. Save the higher control settings for after you have something worth protecting.
