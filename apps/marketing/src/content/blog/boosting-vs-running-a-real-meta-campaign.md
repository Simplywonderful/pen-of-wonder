## The button that makes advertising look simple

There's a Boost Post button on every Facebook and Instagram post you publish. It's designed to be simple: pick a budget, pick a duration, click boost. Meta does the rest.

That simplicity is the problem.

## What boosting actually tells the algorithm

When you boost a post, you're essentially saying: this content is good, I want more people to see it. That's the entire brief. You're not telling Meta what you want those people to *do* after they see it. You're not telling it what a win looks like. You're handing over your budget and letting the platform decide what to optimize for.

Meta will usually optimize for engagement: likes, comments, reach. Those numbers go up. The post looks like it's performing. But engagement isn't the same as leads, and leads aren't the same as sales. If your goal was clients, an engagement-optimized boost won't get you there.

## What a real campaign does differently

An actual ad campaign starts from the other end. You decide the objective first:

- Is the goal to generate leads?
- Drive traffic to a landing page?
- Get purchases?
- Get people to send a message?

Each of those is a different campaign structure, a different bidding strategy, and a different way the algorithm behaves. When you set the objective correctly, Meta knows what a conversion looks like and optimizes toward it: the same distinction that separates a [leads campaign from a sales campaign](/blog/sales-campaigns-vs-leads-campaigns-meta-pixel-tracking).

Then you build the creative. Not a post you already made that performed well, but something specifically designed to achieve that objective: a hook written for cold audiences, a message that speaks to the right person's specific problem, and a call to action that matches the goal.

## Amplifying vs. engineering

Boosting is amplifying something that already exists. Running a campaign is engineering an outcome.

The distinction matters more than most people realize. A boosted post with a large budget can generate thousands of likes from people who will never buy from you. A well-structured campaign with a fraction of that budget can bring in qualified leads from people who actually need what you're offering.

One is about reach. The other is about results.
