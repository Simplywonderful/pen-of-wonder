## The second way to reach people with buying power

Two weeks ago I said there were two ways to target people who can actually afford what you're selling, and that I'd only get into one of them then. [The first was location](/blog/target-buyers-who-can-afford-it-by-location). Here is the second: interest.

## Start with interests wealthy buyers are likely to have

Using the same luxury bags for women example, I'd start listing out the interests wealthy women in that market are likely to have on Facebook. Things like Luxury, Luxury Houses, iPhone, Mary Kay, vacation trips, first class travel, business class travel, and other keywords along those lines. This is the kind of research you actually sit down and do rather than guess at.

As I add these interests, I watch the audience size the same way I did with location, letting it grow into the millions before narrowing anything down.

## Then narrow with Engaged Shoppers

Here's where it gets sharper. In the narrow audience section, I add Engaged Shoppers. A quick correction on something I used to get wrong myself: this is not people who have bought something through Meta before. It's people who clicked Shop Now on an ad within the past week. It doesn't confirm a purchase, but it does confirm someone who actually acts on ads instead of scrolling past them, which is a far better signal than interest on its own.

1. **Location.** Who has the money: wealthy states, then the specific areas inside them.
2. **Interests.** Who is likely to care: luxury, premium travel, and similar interests, added wide first.
3. **Engaged Shoppers.** Who actually responds to ads: added through Narrow Audience as a Must also match condition.

Stack the two together, location for who has the money and Engaged Shoppers for who actually responds to ads, and targeting stops being a guess and starts working like an actual filter.

## The setting that decides whether it narrows or widens

One thing worth adding for anyone testing this: add Engaged Shoppers through the Narrow Audience option so it shows as Must also match. If you drop it in the same box as your interests, Meta treats the items as OR, which widens your audience instead of filtering it. That's the same logic mistake behind [the campaign that was starved of leads by OR logic](/blog/or-logic-vs-and-logic-meta-targeting).

## Same reminder as last time

None of this replaces knowing exactly who you're selling to. Targeting sharpens where your ad lands. It doesn't decide whether your message is right for the person it lands on.
