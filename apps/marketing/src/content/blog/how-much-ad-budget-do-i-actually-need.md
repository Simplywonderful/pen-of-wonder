## The question every business owner asks first

The number one question I get from business owners who haven't run ads before is some version of: how much do I need?

The honest answer is: enough to get real data.

## Why there's no universal number

There's no figure that works for every business. The right budget depends on your offer price, your conversion rate, and how long it takes the average person in your market to make a decision. A business selling a high-ticket service needs more data to make a campaign profitable than a business selling a low-priced impulse buy. Those aren't the same exercise, and treating them the same way is how people conclude that ads don't work.

## What a budget that's too low actually looks like

What I can tell you is what a budget that's too low looks like: it runs out before the algorithm finishes learning. Meta needs enough data to figure out who in your target audience actually converts. Not just who clicks or scrolls, but who does the thing you're paying for. As a rough benchmark, most campaign types need roughly 50 conversion events per week to exit the [learning phase](/blog/why-most-ad-budgets-actually-get-wasted). That number, combined with your expected conversion rate, is what actually tells you the minimum budget worth starting with.

If the budget runs dry before the platform has that information, you haven't run a campaign. You've run a test that stopped before it produced results.

> **~50 events / week**: Roughly what Meta needs in conversion events for most campaign types to exit the learning phase: the real anchor for a minimum budget, not a flat naira figure

## The more important question isn't how much. It's how long

A modest budget run consistently over 60 days will outperform a larger budget pulled at day ten because nothing had happened yet. The algorithm improves over time. A campaign that keeps running keeps learning.

When someone asks me if they have enough to start, I don't answer with a number. I ask how long they can commit to running it without needing it to pay for itself immediately. That answer tells me more than any budget figure.
