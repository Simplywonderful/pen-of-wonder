## Advice built for the wrong funnel

Most of the advice about running ads was written for product businesses. Buy the product, add to cart, complete checkout: a clean funnel, measurable at every step.

If you sell a service, that's not how your buyer thinks. They aren't making a purchase decision. They're making a trust decision. And you can't optimize a trust decision the same way you optimize a product purchase.

## Why service ads "don't work" the way people expect

This is why service businesses consistently feel like ads aren't working for them. They set up a sales campaign, send cold traffic to a landing page, and nobody buys. Of course they don't: [someone who's never heard of you and just scrolled past your ad](/blog/dont-run-sales-campaigns-to-cold-traffic) doesn't trust you enough to pay for a service yet. That trust takes time, and ads can build it, but only if the campaign is set up to do that job.

## The real first job of a service ad

For a service business, the first job of an ad is not to sell. It's to start a conversation:

- A **leads campaign** that collects contact information.
- A **traffic campaign** that sends people to content that shows how you think.
- An **engagement campaign** that builds familiarity before you ask for anything.

The sale comes later, from an audience that's been through that process and is actually ready to say yes. That's a completely different campaign structure from what most service businesses build the first time around.

If you run a coaching practice, a consulting business, or any service that requires trust before it can be sold, paid ads can work for you. They just need to be configured for the right objective.
