## What most people expect vs. what actually happens

Most people think hiring a paid ads specialist means handing over a budget and waiting for leads. That's not how I work.

1. **Audit the offer.** Who is it for? What problem does it solve? Has anyone paid for it without being nudged by someone they know?
2. **Build the infrastructure.** Conversion tracking, the pixel, and the events that tell Meta when something meaningful has actually happened. Not just a click.
3. **Launch with real variation.** Not one ad. Enough creative variations (different hooks, angles, and ways into the same message) for the algorithm to have something real to test.
4. **Give it 30 days.** The first 30 days are a learning phase for the platform, not the verdict. My job is to read the data and adjust, not judge early.

## Step one: the offer audit

The first thing I do before any campaign goes live is not touch the ads account. It's an audit of the offer. Who is it for? What is the one problem it solves? Has anyone paid for it without being nudged by someone they know? These questions aren't gatekeeping: [the answers tell me whether we're building on solid ground](/blog/three-questions-before-i-run-your-ads) or just spending money faster.

## Step two: infrastructure before spend

Then we set up the infrastructure: conversion tracking, the pixel, the events that tell Meta when something meaningful has happened, not just a click, but the actual action the campaign is built to produce. This is not optional. [Running ads without proper tracking is spending money and hoping](/blog/sales-campaigns-vs-leads-campaigns-meta-pixel-tracking).

## Step three: enough creative to actually test

Then the campaign goes live. Not with one ad, but with enough variations to give the algorithm something real to work with. Different hooks, different angles, different ways into the same core message. The algorithm needs options. Without them, it can't learn, and a campaign that can't learn can't improve.

## Step four: let the learning phase run

The first 30 days are a learning phase. Not for me, for the platform. My job during that period is to read what the data is telling me and adjust what needs adjusting before drawing any conclusions about what's working and what isn't. [Most campaigns that fail do so because someone read the results before the data was ready to give them](/blog/why-most-ad-budgets-actually-get-wasted).

I don't make that mistake. And I don't let clients make it either.
