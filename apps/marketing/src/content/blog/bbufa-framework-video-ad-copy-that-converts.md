## Structure beats polish

Most ad copy doesn't fail because it's badly written. It fails because it's missing pieces the buyer needed before they could say yes.

There's a structure to a video ad that converts cold traffic. When the structure is right, the writing almost doesn't matter. When the structure is wrong, even beautifully written copy won't save it. I've been testing a framework that maps exactly what needs to be present and in what order. It's called BBUFA.

1. **Big Promise.** Specific enough to be believable, ambitious enough to make them want it. Earns the next thirty seconds of attention.
2. **Backup.** Evidence behind the claim: numbers, results, track record. Not hype.
3. **USP.** What separates your offer from every alternative the viewer has already tried, in a way that actually matters to them.
4. **FOMO.** A real reason to decide now instead of later. If it isn't real, don't say it: manufactured urgency gets spotted and kills trust.
5. **Action.** A specific instruction for exactly what to do next. Not 'learn more.'

## What each element is actually doing

**Big Promise.** This is the first thing your viewer needs to hear, and it needs to be specific enough to be believable and ambitious enough to make them want it. Not "I will help you grow your business." Something like: "I'm going to show you the exact system I used to take a client from three leads a month to forty, without increasing their ad spend." The promise has to be big enough to earn the next thirty seconds of their attention.

**Backup.** The big promise raises a question immediately: why should I believe you? The backup is where you answer it. Numbers, results, testimonials, track record: anything that puts evidence behind the claim. Not hype. Evidence.

**Unique Selling Point.** What makes this different from everything else the viewer has already tried or seen? The USP isn't a feature list. It's the one thing that separates your offer from the alternatives in a way that actually matters to your buyer.

**Fear of Missing Out.** Scarcity and urgency, when they're real, do genuine work at this point in the video. The viewer is interested but still sitting on the fence. FOMO creates a reason to decide now rather than later. If it's not real, don't say it: manufactured urgency gets spotted and it kills trust.

**Action.** Tell them exactly what to do next. Not "learn more." Not "visit our website." A specific instruction: "Click the link below, fill in your details, and I'll personally reach out within 24 hours to confirm your spot."

## Why the order matters

That sequence (promise, proof, differentiation, urgency, instruction) is what a converting video ad looks like underneath the creative. The format can change. The hook can vary. But when these five elements are present in the right order, the viewer has everything they need to make a decision.

The element most people skip is the backup. They make the big promise and jump straight to the USP, assuming the viewer already trusts them enough to keep watching. They don't. The proof is what buys you the next thirty seconds.
