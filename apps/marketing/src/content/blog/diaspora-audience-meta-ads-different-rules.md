## A different game, not a bigger-budget version of the same one

Running ads for a diaspora audience is not the Nigerian playbook translated into a different currency. It's a different game with different rules, and treating it like a bigger-budget version of what already works locally is how people lose money fast.

## Start with cost, because the benchmarks move

CPC, ROAS, and CPA all sit at different levels once you're targeting Nigerians abroad. Competition for attention in those markets is higher, cost per click is usually higher, and what counts as a good return shifts with it. Walk in expecting Nigerian benchmarks and you'll either panic at the numbers or misjudge what's actually a strong result.

## The real risk isn't cost. It's the promise

Cost is the foundation, but it's not where the real risk sits. The bigger shift is in what you promise and how you deliver on it.

Your copy, your creative, and your offer all need to change for a diaspora audience, because the person you're speaking to has different expectations shaped by the country they live in now. And this is the part that matters most: you have to actually be able to deliver exactly what you promise.

In Nigeria, a business can over-promise and under-deliver and often walk away with only mild consequences. That's not a defense of the practice. It's genuinely one of the more damaging habits I see in local business, and it costs long-term trust even when it looks like it costs nothing short-term. With a diaspora audience, that margin doesn't exist. One dissatisfied customer can cost you far more than the sale itself.

If you can't deliver exactly what you're promising, you have no business selling to that market. Not because the money isn't good: because the cost of getting it wrong is disproportionate to what you gain from getting it right.

## Targeting carries more weight, not less

On top of all this, targeting carries more weight than it does locally. A diaspora audience isn't one block of people. Country, income level, how long they've lived abroad, and how connected they still are to home all change who you should actually be targeting and how: the same [interests, behaviors, and demographics stack](/blog/interests-behaviors-demographics-meta-targeting-layers) applies, but the assumptions underneath each layer need rebuilding for this audience.

If you're thinking about running ads to a diaspora audience, do the cost and compliance checks before you touch the creative. The creative is not where this campaign succeeds or fails first.
