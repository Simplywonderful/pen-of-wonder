Running Meta ads for a coaching business or an online course comes down to five decisions: which objective you choose, where you send people, how many creatives you test, how you track the leads, and how long you wait before you judge the results. Get those five right and a small budget can teach you a lot. Get them wrong and the same budget buys likes.

This guide walks through each one in the order I set them up for a client.

## In short

- Choose the objective that matches the action you want: usually **Leads** or **Sales**, not Engagement.
- Send people to one place that can turn interest into a conversation: an instant form, WhatsApp or a landing page.
- Launch with several creatives and copy variations, never one.
- Track every lead back to the ad that brought it.
- Leave a new ad set alone for about a week. Meta's system needs that time to learn.

## What does a coach need before running Meta ads?

You need an offer people already buy, a way to reply quickly, and enough creative to test. I've written about these in more detail in [What you need in place before you spend on paid ads](/blog/what-you-need-before-spending-on-paid-ads), so here is the short version.

Your offer should have sold to real clients without ads. Ads make a working offer reach more people. They don't fix an offer nobody wants.

You also need someone ready to answer when the leads arrive. A coaching client who messages you at 9pm and hears nothing until the next afternoon has usually moved on.

## Which campaign objective should you choose?

Meta Ads Manager has six campaign objectives: Awareness, Traffic, Engagement, Leads, App promotion and Sales. The objective tells Meta which people to look for, so it shapes who sees your ad.

| Objective | What Meta looks for | When a coach should use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness | People most likely to remember your ad | Rarely, on a small budget |
| Traffic | People likely to click through to a page | When you only want visits, not contacts |
| Engagement | People likely to like, comment or message | For messages, chosen carefully |
| Leads | People likely to share their details | Discovery calls, free sessions, webinars |
| App promotion | People likely to install an app | Only if you sell through an app |
| Sales | People likely to buy | When people pay on your website |

For most coaches and course creators, the choice is between **Leads** and **Sales**. If people book a call or message you before they pay, use Leads. If they can buy a course on your website, use Sales, with the Meta pixel installed so Meta can see the purchases.

The common mistake is choosing Engagement, or [boosting a post](/blog/boosting-vs-running-a-real-meta-campaign), because the results look good. Meta's own description says Engagement finds people likely to interact with your ad or Page. That's what you'll get: people who interact, not people who pay.

## Where should the ad send people?

Send people to one place, and make it a place that starts a conversation. You have three sensible options:

1. **Instant forms.** The form opens inside Facebook or Instagram, so it's quick to fill in. You get more leads, but some are less serious, because it costs the person very little effort.
2. **WhatsApp or Messenger.** People start a chat with you straight from the ad. This suits coaches, because the sale usually happens in conversation.
3. **A landing page.** One page about one offer, with one action. It takes more effort to build, but you control what people read before they contact you, and you can track them properly.

Whichever you choose, don't send ad traffic to your homepage. If you need a page built for one offer, that's what our [landing page service](/landing-pages) is for. A homepage gives people ten things to do, and they'll do none of them.

## How many creatives should you launch with?

Launch with three to five images and three to five short videos, each with a few headline and copy variations. That's the floor, not the target; on my own ads [I run at least twenty](/blog/why-you-need-twenty-ad-creatives-not-three).

The reason is simple: you don't know in advance which message will land. One creative gives Meta nothing to compare. Several give it room to find the version your audience responds to. Once the campaign is running, keep adding new angles so it always has something fresh to test.

For coaches, the angles that are worth testing first are:

- **The problem**: name the situation your client is stuck in, in their own words.
- **The result**: what changes for them after working with you.
- **The objection**: answer the reason they've been putting it off, such as time, money or past failures.
- **You**: a short video of you explaining one useful idea. People buy coaching from a person.

## How do you know which ad brought each lead?

Install the Meta pixel on your website and set up the Conversions API, then tag every ad link with UTM parameters. Meta describes the pixel as a piece of code on your website that measures the actions people take after seeing your ads. The Conversions API sends the same kind of data from your server, which helps when browsers block the pixel.

If your leads come through WhatsApp, ask every new contact where they saw you, and write it down. It's low-tech, but it works. A lead you can't trace back to an ad is a lead you can't learn from. I explain the setup step by step in [Ad tracking for small businesses](/blog/ad-tracking-pixel-conversions-api-and-utms).

## How long should you wait before judging the results?

Wait about a week. Meta says an ad set usually leaves its learning phase after about 50 results in the week after its last significant edit. During that time results swing up and down, and costs are usually higher.

Every significant edit restarts the clock. Changing the budget sharply, swapping the creative or narrowing the audience all count. So set the campaign up carefully, then leave it alone for seven days unless something is clearly broken.

I explain my own rule of thumb in [How long should you let a Meta ad run before judging it?](/blog/how-long-to-let-a-meta-ad-run-before-judging-it)

If you can't afford enough results in a week to get out of learning, Meta will mark the ad set "Learning limited". That's a sign to combine ad sets or raise the budget, not to keep editing.

## A simple first-month plan

| Week | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Before launch | Offer proven, reply plan ready, pixel and UTMs set up, five or more creatives made |
| Week 1 | Launch one Leads campaign with one ad set and all your creatives. Don't touch it |
| Week 2 | Read cost per lead and lead quality. Pause the clear losers, keep the rest |
| Week 3 | Add new creatives built on the angle that worked |
| Week 4 | Decide whether to raise the budget, based on how many leads became clients |

The last row matters most. Cost per lead tells you what you paid. The number of leads who became paying clients tells you whether it was worth paying. To set your own target, see [What is a good cost per lead?](/blog/what-is-a-good-cost-per-lead)
