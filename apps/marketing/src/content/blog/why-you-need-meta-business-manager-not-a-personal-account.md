## One restriction away from losing everything

If you're running ads from your personal Facebook account, you're one restriction away from losing everything you've built in that account overnight. I've seen this happen.

## Two people, two personal accounts, one business that couldn't run ads

A digital marketing director at an e-commerce company was running ads through the company's setup. The CEO's account got restricted: for reasons that were never entirely clear, possibly the products, possibly repeated reports from people seeing the ads. When the CEO's account went down, the director switched to his own personal Facebook account to keep things running. That account was also restricted shortly after.

Two people. Two personal accounts. One business that could no longer run ads at all.

## Why a personal account is a structural problem

That's the structural problem with using a personal account. When something goes wrong (and on Meta, something eventually always does), there's no separation between you and your business. The account that holds your connections, your memories, your personal history is the same one Meta can restrict because of ad activity. You lose everything at once.

## What Meta Business Manager actually solves

Meta Business Manager was built to solve this. It's a separate interface designed specifically for running a business on Meta's platforms. It gives you a business portfolio that exists independently of your personal profile. You can manage your Facebook Page, your Instagram account, your ad account, your pixel, and your team access all in one dashboard, without any of it being connected to your personal timeline.

It's also simply a better tool. You can schedule content weeks in advance. You can monitor messages and comments across Facebook and Instagram in one place. You can give team members or clients access without sharing your personal login. Everything a business needs to manage its presence on Meta is there, built for that exact purpose.
