## Why does the first reply to an ad lead matter so much?

You paid to get that lead. Two weeks ago I said you need [a way to respond the moment someone shows interest](/blog/what-you-need-before-spending-on-paid-ads). This post is what that looks like in practice. It comes from my own experience, not from a course.

A lead who messages and gets no answer, or a flat generic one, is money gone. The ad did its job. The reply decides what happens next.

## Who should answer the first message?

Someone should be on standby, especially for the first message. If no one can be, consider a bot trained well enough to answer naturally.

People arrive with different needs and questions, and an ad rarely answers them all. Automated replies built on triggers can work, but people have got used to them. They spot them, and some will ask a follow-up question just to check whether a real person is there. If nobody answers when a serious buyer does that, you've lost them.

## What should the first message say?

It should do two things: speak to the problem they came to solve, and qualify them. Not a generic opener. A question that filters out the people who aren't ready to buy.

You can do a good job on copy, targeting and creative and still reach people who can't afford to buy. The first message is where you find out. A good qualifying question is one that only a serious buyer will bother to answer properly.

## When should you ask for a call instead of staying on chat?

For a service or a higher-priced offer, once someone is qualified, ask for a call. Ask what time suits them and make sure it happens.

1. **Be on standby.** Someone answers the first message, or a well-trained bot does if no one can.
2. **Qualify in the first reply.** Speak to the problem they came to solve and ask a question that filters out people who aren't ready to buy.
3. **Ask for a call.** Once they're qualified, ask what time suits them and make sure the call happens.

On a call, their questions and objections tell you quickly whether they're a real prospect, and you can get a commitment instead of going back and forth in chat where many people go quiet.

## What is the common mistake with ad leads?

Keeping everything on chat and following up again and again, hoping someone will reply. Anyone who really wants to stay on chat should be willing to promise they'll reply promptly.

If you chase 100 leads by chat, you'll waste a lot of time. Call a few, see who is qualified, and put your follow-up where it will count.

## How does this fit with tracking and ad performance?

A booked call is only useful if you record what happens next. The pipeline in [tracking leads past the point your ad platform stops watching](/blog/track-leads-past-the-point-your-ad-platform-stops-watching) is built around exactly that: qualify, book a call, log the outcome. For a service business in particular, [an ad's first job is trust, not the sale](/blog/service-businesses-ads-are-a-trust-decision), which is why the first reply matters so much.
