## It's not the audience. It's the expectation.

The most common reason businesses waste money on ads is not what most people think. It isn't the wrong audience. It isn't a bad product. It isn't even the creative, though that matters more than most people realize.

It's expectations: specifically, what someone has been led to believe will happen the moment a campaign goes live.

Most business owners start running ads believing results should appear within days, sometimes hours. If leads aren't coming by day three, something must be wrong. If sales aren't there by the end of week one, ads "don't work" for their business. That is not how it works.

## What actually happens when a campaign launches

When you launch a Meta campaign, the algorithm doesn't already know who your buyer is. It starts testing. It needs data, real conversion data, to figure out which people in your target audience will actually do the thing you're asking them to do. That process takes time, and it takes budget, and it takes enough events firing correctly for the platform to learn.

If you cut the campaign before that learning phase completes, you haven't discovered that ads don't work for your business. You've discovered that you stopped before the system had what it needed to function. The expectation was wrong. In most cases, the setup was wrong too.

## The setup mistakes that compound the problem

A sales campaign is not the same as a leads campaign. They're built differently, they optimize differently, and they require different tracking in place before you spend anything. Three specific mistakes show up again and again:

- **Running the wrong objective** because it sounded right, rather than because it matches what the offer actually needs.
- **Launching with one creative** instead of several, which means the algorithm has nothing real to test against.
- **Setting up without conversion tracking**, which means you're spending money and flying completely blind on what's actually working.

Running the wrong objective is a budget leak most businesses never identify, because the campaign still looks like it's "running": impressions climb, the dashboard has numbers on it. The numbers just aren't the ones that matter.

## What actually changes the outcome

None of this is complicated, but it has to happen in the right order:

1. **Right objective.** Match the campaign objective to what you're actually selling.
2. **Tracking verified.** Conversion tracking set up and verified before a single naira goes in.
3. **Enough creative.** Enough variations to give the algorithm genuine options to test.
4. **Enough time.** Let the learning phase run its course before judging results.
5. **Honest measurement.** Read what the data is actually telling you, not what you hoped it would say.

Ads are a system. When the system is configured correctly and given time to learn, results become predictable. When it isn't, the budget disappears and the conclusion becomes "ads don't work." That conclusion is almost never right: the setup or the patience usually was.
