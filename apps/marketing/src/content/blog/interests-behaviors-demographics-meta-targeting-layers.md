## Why interest targeting feels right but isn't enough

Interest targeting feels like the logical place to start. You know who your ideal customer is, you match their interests to Meta's options, and you let the platform find them. The logic is clean.

The problem is that interests are a lagging signal.

## Interests are based on the past, not the present

When Meta tags someone as interested in entrepreneurship or digital marketing, that classification is based on their past behavior online: pages they liked, content they engaged with, links they clicked. What someone was engaged with six months ago isn't necessarily what they're actively pursuing or spending money on today. People's interests shift. The algorithm's data doesn't always keep up.

If your targeting relies entirely on interests, you're fishing in a pool that includes active buyers, passive browsers, former enthusiasts, and people who clicked something once and moved on. Those groups behave very differently in response to an ad, and you can't separate them with interest targeting alone.

## Three layers, combined

What builds a more precise audience is combining three layers:

1. **Interests.** What someone has engaged with: a real signal, but a lagging one that can be months out of date.
2. **Behaviors.** What someone is actively doing right now: a more current signal than interests.
3. **Demographics.** Age, location, income bracket: the structural filter that narrows to who could realistically buy.

Behaviors are a more current signal. They reflect what someone is actively doing, not what they were browsing last year. Job titles and seniority are more stable identifiers than interests, since a person's role changes much more slowly than their browsing habits.

Demographics provide the structural filter: age, location, income bracket narrow the audience to people whose actual situation matches the profile of someone who'd buy your offer.

## Why AND logic, not OR, is what makes this work

The combination of all three, applied with [AND logic rather than OR](/blog/or-logic-vs-and-logic-meta-targeting), creates a meaningful filter. You're no longer reaching everyone who matches any one condition. You're reaching people who match across multiple dimensions simultaneously: the relevant interests, the right behaviors, and the right demographic profile. That intersection is a much hotter audience than any single layer on its own.

A broad audience generates impressions. A precise audience generates the kind of data that actually tells you whether your campaign is working.
