Boosting a post is the quickest way to put money behind something you've already published, and it is a real ad. But it gives you fewer goals, fewer placements and less control than a campaign built in Meta Ads Manager. Boost when you want a good post seen by more people. Build a campaign when you need leads or sales you can trace to the money you spent.

## In short

- Meta says boosted posts are paid ads, but ads made in Ads Manager or Meta Business Suite "offer more customisation options".
- Boosting starts from a post you've already published. A campaign starts from a goal.
- Boosted posts can't use objectives such as Leads, App promotion or Sales. Ads Manager can.
- Use boosting for reach and conversation. Use a campaign when you're paying for results you need to count.

## What is a boosted post?

A boosted post is an ad made from something you already published on your Facebook Page or Instagram account: a post, a story or a reel. You press the Boost button, choose a goal, an audience, a budget and a duration, and Meta shows the post to more people.

When you boost, Meta lets you decide on five things: your goal, the ad text, the schedule, the budget and the placements. That's enough to get a post in front of more people. It's not enough to run a proper test.

## What can a campaign do that boosting can't?

A campaign built in Meta Ads Manager can do four things a boosted post can't, and each one matters when you're paying for leads.

| | Boosted post | Campaign in Ads Manager |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starting point | A post you already published | A goal you choose first |
| Objectives | Website clicks, Page engagement, local promotion, messages | All six, including Leads and Sales |
| Placements | Facebook and Instagram feeds, mostly | Also Stories, Messenger, WhatsApp status, Audience Network and more |
| Creative | The post as it is, with optional text variations | Several images, videos, carousels and copy variations in one ad set |
| Testing | Hard to compare versions | Built for comparing creatives and audiences |

The objective is the big one. Meta's system looks for the people most likely to do what you asked for. Ask for engagement and you'll get likes and comments. Ask for leads and it looks for people likely to share their details. If your boosted post brought plenty of reactions and no clients, this is usually why. I compare all six objectives in [Meta ads for coaches and course creators](/blog/meta-ads-for-coaches-and-course-creators).

## When is boosting the right choice?

Boosting is the right choice when the goal really is attention or conversation. Three situations where I'd boost without hesitating:

1. **A post is already doing well.** It's getting comments and shares from the right people. A small boost lets more of them see it.
2. **You want messages.** A boosted post with a "Send message" button can start WhatsApp or Messenger chats, which suits businesses that sell in conversation.
3. **You're announcing something.** An event, a new location, a launch date. You want people to know, not necessarily to act today.

## When should you build a campaign instead?

Build a campaign whenever you need to count what you got for your money. That includes:

- collecting leads for a discovery call, a free class or a consultation
- selling a product or a course on your website
- testing which message or image works best
- retargeting people who visited your website or watched your videos

All four need things boosting doesn't give you: the Leads or Sales objective, several creatives in one ad set, and [tracking that ties each result back to an ad](/blog/ad-tracking-pixel-conversions-api-and-utms).

## Why boosted posts often look good and sell little

Boosted posts often look like they're working. The reactions roll in, the reach number climbs, and the post feels popular. Then nobody buys.

That's not a fault in boosting. It's doing what you asked. If the goal you chose was engagement, Meta found people who engage. They're often not the people who pay.

The fix isn't to stop boosting forever. It's to be honest about what each boost is for. If it's for reach, judge it on reach. If you need clients, move that money into a campaign with the Leads or Sales objective, and judge it on how many leads became paying clients.

## A quick way to decide

Ask one question before you spend: **what do I need this money to bring back?**

- More people seeing a post I'm proud of: **boost it**.
- More conversations on WhatsApp or Messenger: **boost it, or run a campaign with messages as the goal**.
- Leads I can count and follow up: **run a campaign**.
- Sales I can trace to a specific ad: **run a campaign, with the pixel installed**.

If you're not sure what you need it to bring back, don't spend it yet.
