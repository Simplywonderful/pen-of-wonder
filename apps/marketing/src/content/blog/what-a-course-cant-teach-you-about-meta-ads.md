## Confidence built from study, tested by a real account

I'd spent months studying how Meta ads work: courses, documentation, platform walkthroughs, until I could explain the structure from memory. I understood the concepts. I was confident.

Then I ran a campaign that spent real money and generated zero paying customers in three weeks.

## What the failure actually taught

That failure taught me more than any module on campaign optimization. It taught me where the actual gaps are. Not between what you know and what you've studied, but between what the interface shows you and what's happening underneath it. The audience size looked fine. The targeting looked organized. Nothing flagged as wrong. The logic running underneath was [quietly pulling in the wrong people for weeks](/blog/or-logic-vs-and-logic-meta-targeting).

You can't simulate that in a course. You can read about OR logic and AND logic and understand the distinction completely. The lesson that sticks is the one where you open a real account, look at a real audience of 5.4 million people, and realize the campaign has been showing your ad to the wrong segment of your market the entire time.

## Courses build the model. Campaigns test it.

Courses build the mental model. Campaigns test whether the mental model actually holds. In my experience, the model usually holds in broad strokes and breaks down in the specific details the interface doesn't make obvious.

That gap between theory and practice is where I do my most useful work. If you've run ads before and hit a wall you couldn't diagnose, that's exactly the kind of problem worth solving.
