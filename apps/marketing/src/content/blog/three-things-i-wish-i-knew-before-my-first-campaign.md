## The unpolished list

Everyone who's run a Meta campaign has a list of things they wish someone had told them before they started. This is mine. Not the polished version, the actual gaps that showed up in real campaigns and cost real budget before I understood what was happening.

1. **Targeting logic.** A single targeting box reads as OR logic, not AND: the audience balloons and nothing in the interface flags it as wrong.
2. **Campaign objectives.** A leads campaign and a sales campaign aren't the same setup with different labels. They're fundamentally different structures.
3. **Time and resources.** A small budget and a handful of creatives hit a ceiling before the algorithm has learned enough to make good decisions.

## Lesson one: targeting logic

The first is about targeting logic. When I set up my first few campaigns, I put everything into a single targeting box and assumed the audience was well defined. It looked organized. It wasn't. Every interest sitting in that box was connected by OR logic, meaning the platform would show the ad to anyone who matched any one condition. The audience ballooned to a size that had no business sitting against the daily budget running on that campaign. The campaign ran, people messaged, and almost nothing converted.

When I finally went looking for the problem and [switched to AND logic using Meta's Narrow Audience function](/blog/or-logic-vs-and-logic-meta-targeting), leads rose sharply within days and the cost per lead fell to a fraction of what it had been. Nothing else changed: the targeting logic was the only variable.

The fix took about ten minutes once I found it. Nothing in the interface flagged it as wrong. The ad was running, the metrics looked like something was happening: the problem was invisible until I went looking for it specifically.

## Lesson two: objectives aren't interchangeable labels

The second is about objectives. I didn't fully understand that [choosing the wrong campaign objective](/blog/wrong-campaign-objective-awareness-vs-engagement) doesn't just affect the result. It affects what the algorithm optimizes for, what tracking infrastructure needs to be in place before launch, and what the data means when you read it afterward. A leads campaign and a sales campaign aren't the same setup with different labels. They're fundamentally different structures. Running the wrong one means the system is learning toward the wrong outcome from day one, and no amount of creative testing will fix a misconfigured objective.

## Lesson three: time and resources have a floor

The third is about time and resources. I wish I'd understood earlier that a small budget has a ceiling, and that ceiling arrives before the algorithm has seen enough to make good decisions. Meta needs data, real conversion events, before it knows who in your audience is actually likely to buy. If the budget runs out before that threshold is reached, the campaign didn't fail. It just stopped before it could learn anything useful. The same applies to creative: a handful of ads gives the algorithm almost nothing to work with. The more variations you test, the more signal you generate, and the faster the system finds what actually works.

None of these are complicated in hindsight. They just require someone to tell you before you find out the expensive way.
