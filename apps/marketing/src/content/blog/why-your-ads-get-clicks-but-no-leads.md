If your ads are getting clicks but no leads, the ad is usually not the problem. Clicks mean the ad caught attention and earned a visit. Something after the click is letting people down: the page, the form, the speed, the campaign setting or the tracking. Check those five, in that order, before you rewrite a single ad.

## In short

- Clicks without leads is almost always an after-the-click problem.
- The most common cause is a page that doesn't match what the ad promised.
- Long forms, slow pages and a Traffic objective are the next three suspects.
- Sometimes the leads are coming in and you're just not counting them. Check that before you panic.

## 1. Does the page match the ad?

Start here, because it's the most common cause and the cheapest to fix. (If you'd rather have the page built for you, see our [landing page service](/landing-pages).) Someone clicked because the ad promised something specific. If the page they land on says something different, or something vaguer, they leave.

Open your ad and your landing page side by side and check three things:

- **The headline.** Does the page's first line repeat the promise in the ad, in close to the same words?
- **The offer.** If the ad said "free 20-minute call", is that what the page offers, right at the top?
- **The look.** Does the page look like it belongs to the same business as the ad, with similar colours, images and tone?

If someone has to scroll or search to find the thing the ad promised, most won't.

## 2. Is the form asking for too much?

Every field you add is a reason to stop. A form that asks for name, email, phone, company, budget, timeline and a paragraph about the problem will get fewer completions than one that asks for name and WhatsApp number.

Ask only for what you need for the next step. If the next step is a WhatsApp chat, you need a name and a number. You can ask the rest in the conversation, where it feels like a conversation rather than a test.

## 3. Is the page fast enough on a phone?

Most people who click a Facebook or Instagram ad are on a phone, often on mobile data. If the page takes too long to load, they've already tapped back before it appears. Ad platforms usually count that as a click, and you never see the person on your page.

Test your landing page on your own phone, on mobile data, not on your office Wi-Fi. If you're waiting, so are they. The usual culprits are large images, video that plays on load, and page builders that load far more than the page needs.

## 4. Did you choose the right campaign objective?

If the campaign uses the **Traffic** objective, Meta is doing exactly what you asked: finding people likely to click. Meta describes Traffic as sending people to a destination such as your website. It doesn't promise they'll do anything once they get there.

If you want leads, use the **Leads** objective, or **Sales** if people buy on your site. I go through [a real first campaign with the wrong objective](/blog/why-your-first-meta-ads-campaign-isnt-getting-sign-ups) step by step. Then Meta looks for people likely to take that action, not just people likely to tap.

## 5. Are the leads arriving but not being counted?

Before you change anything, check that leads aren't getting lost on the way to you. I've seen all of these:

- the form sends submissions to an email inbox nobody checks
- the thank-you page has no tracking, so Meta never sees the lead
- the Meta pixel is on the homepage but not on the landing page
- WhatsApp leads arrive, but nobody records which ad they came from

Fill in your own form, from your own ad, on your phone. Then check that the lead reached the right inbox and that Ads Manager counted it. If either one fails, fix that first. You can't improve results you can't see. [Ad tracking for small businesses](/blog/ad-tracking-pixel-conversions-api-and-utms) shows how to set it up properly.

## A checklist to run before you touch the ad

| Check | How to test it | Fix if it fails |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Page matches the ad | Read the ad, then the first screen of the page | Rewrite the page headline to repeat the ad's promise |
| Form is short | Count the fields | Cut to what the next step needs |
| Page is fast | Open it on your phone on mobile data | Shrink images, remove autoplay video, simplify the page |
| Objective fits the goal | Check the campaign in Ads Manager | Use Leads or Sales, not Traffic |
| Leads are counted | Submit a test lead from the ad | Fix the form's destination and the tracking |

## When is it the ad after all?

Sometimes the ad really is the problem, but not in the way people expect. An ad can get lots of clicks by being too broad or too clever: a curious headline that draws in people who have no need for what you sell. They click, they see the page, they realise it isn't for them, and they leave.

If the five checks above all pass and you're still getting clicks without leads, make the ad more specific. Name who it's for and what it costs, or say plainly what happens next. You'll get fewer clicks, and more of them will be the right people.
