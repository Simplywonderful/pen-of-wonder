## Why I ask before I build anything

Before I agree to run ads for anyone, I ask them three questions. Not because I'm being difficult: because the answers tell me whether paid advertising is actually the right move for them right now, or whether we'd both be wasting time and money.

## The first question: can you describe the offer in one sentence?

Can you describe what you're selling in one sentence, and does someone who's never heard of you understand it immediately? If the answer is no, ads won't fix that. Ads put your message in front of strangers. If the message is unclear, you're paying to confuse more people faster.

This is the question most business owners underestimate. A confusing offer doesn't get clearer because more people see it. It just gets ignored by more people, at a cost.

## The second question: has the market actually proven it wants this?

Has the market actually shown interest in this, and has anyone paid for it? Not people you imagined would love it. Not friends who said it sounds great. Real evidence, from people who had no obligation to say yes.

Some business owners skip this step entirely. They build the product, decide people will want it, hear that ads can help them reach more people, and go straight to launching campaigns. But [ads don't create demand. They amplify what already exists](/blog/why-most-ad-budgets-actually-get-wasted). If the demand isn't there, ads will only help you find that out faster, at a cost.

## The third question: can you commit to 60 days?

Can you commit to spending consistently for at least 60 days without needing the campaign to pay for itself in week two? The algorithm needs time to learn. Pulling a campaign at day ten because nothing happened yet isn't a failed campaign. It's an incomplete one.

1. **Offer clarity.** Can a total stranger understand what you're selling in one sentence?
2. **Proof of demand.** Has the market actually paid for this already, from people with no reason to say yes otherwise?
3. **Budget commitment.** Can you fund the campaign consistently for at least 60 days, without needing it to pay for itself in week two?

## What happens with the answers

If all three answers are solid, we can have a real conversation. If one isn't there yet, I'll tell you plainly what needs to be in place first. That's not me turning down work. It's making sure the work is worth doing.
